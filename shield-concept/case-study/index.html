Xfinity Shield: earning the upgrade by fixing the free tier first

Shield bundles network security, parental controls and WiFi motion sensing into the Xfinity app for free, with a $15 a month tier that adds a camera and sensor. I looked at what stops people trusting the free tier, redesigned those flows, and then designed an upgrade path that asks at the right moment.

At a glance

Fix the free tier, then earn the upgrade

The problem
People aren’t asking Shield for more features. Reviews describe being locked out, lost in the app, or unsure what it promises, and about 1 in 5 recent App Store reviews describe dead ends with support.
What I did
Read 1,500 App Store reviews across Xfinity, Ring and SimpliSafe plus Play Store reviews, walked through the live app, and compared five rivals. Then I redesigned 18 screens across access, setup, family controls, alerts and help, and designed an upgrade path to Shield Select.
How I’d measure it
North star: the share of eligible households with WiFi Shield still active after 30 days. Guardrails: support contacts, people turning alerts off, and cancellations soon after upgrading.

Context

A retention product inside a billing app

Comcast launched Xfinity Shield on August 18, 2026. The free tier, WiFi Shield, comes with internet plans that use an Advanced Gateway. Shield Select adds an indoor camera with person, pet, vehicle and package detection, a door sensor, cloud video and emergency help for $15 a month.

Coverage of the launch frames it as a response to broadband customers leaving. That makes the design problem two-sided: the free tier has to be good enough to keep people, and credible enough that paying for more feels safe.

Research

Three inputs, one pattern

I read Play Store reviews from August to November 2026 and 500 App Store reviews from October 27 to November 17, walked through the live app on November 18 and captured 28 screens, and compared Shield with five rival apps. The pattern across all three: people are not asking for more features. They are locked out, lost, or unsure what the product promises.

Locked out

An "update required" screen returns after updating, with no newer build in the store. Sign-in loops and error pages block bill pay.

Reviews

Parental controls moved

After the August update, pausing a child's WiFi went from "2 clicks" to "closer to 7", and paused devices don't show as paused.

Reviews

Promise versus fine print

The Home tab says "A forcefield for your home". Setup then asks you to confirm it "is not professionally monitored home security".

Walkthrough

Alerts you can't stop

One reviewer gets "no activity" alerts with every setting off. Another gets a new-device alert each time the same phone reconnects. On iOS, a November update removed the camera preview from lock-screen alerts.

Reviews · both stores

No route to a person

The assistant loops on fixed prompts and, during an outage, blocks other questions and agent access. On iOS: "Day 2 of issues the AI bot can't fix and no human tech support to talk to."

Reviews · both stores

Fragile setup

"Don't leave or close the app or you may need to start over", for a step that takes a few minutes.

  • The Gateway is already in the home, so the free tier needs no new hardware.
  • Motion sensing without cameras.
Weaknesses
  • App stability and navigation dominate reviews.

Opportunities
  • Most ISPs have left home security.

What this means as Shield grows its tiers

Fix the layer around the product first

Support dead ends and connectivity are where Xfinity stands out from Ring. That's the case for the help and app-access fixes coming before new features.

All three apps

Design for camera problems before they arrive

Once cameras are involved, video and alerts dominate complaints, around 40% for Ring and SimpliSafe. As Shield Select grows, fast live view and well-tuned alerts will matter most.

Ring · SimpliSafe

Keep the free tier useful on its own

A Ring reviewer titled theirs "Useless Without the Subscription": 30 motion alerts in 20 minutes with motion settings as low as they go, and without a subscription the only fix is turning alerts off. Basics like alert tuning shouldn't sit behind a paywall.

Ring

Make leaving as easy as upgrading

SimpliSafe reviewers object to calling to cancel and not being able to change plans. Easy downgrades are a trust signal, not lost revenue.

SimpliSafe

Keep marketing out of security alerts

"App is now pushing marketing messages with no way to turn off. Good way to get me to rip it out and put in a Ring system." Upgrade offers belong at moments of value, never in push notifications.

SimpliSafe

Status alerts are noise

SimpliSafe users get "lost connection" and "reconnected" messages all day. Group them, or alert only when it changes what someone should do.

SimpliSafe

Design principles

Six rules I held every screen to

  1. Never lock people out of essentials. Paying a bill, checking an outage and pausing WiFi work even when an update is pending.
  2. Say what it is and what it isn't, first. The "not monitored" message belongs on the opening screen, not behind a checkbox.
  3. Two taps for what parents do daily. Pause, resume and bonus time sit on the Home tab.
  4. Every alert has an off switch and a next step. Act from the notification, and undo it if you tapped wrong.
  5. A person is always reachable. The assistant is one option, never the gate.
  6. Ask for the upgrade when it's worth something. And make "no" as easy as "yes".

Measuring it

How I’d know it’s working

This is a concept, so there are no results. These are the measures I would set up before shipping any of it.

North star: the share of eligible households with WiFi Shield still active after 30 days. It shows people trust the free tier enough to keep it, which is what keeps them as broadband customers and what every upgrade grows from.

FlowMeasureWhy it matters
UpgradeTrial starts from the motion-event prompt versus the Home tab offer; trial-to-paid rate; cancellations in the first weekShows whether asking in context converts better without creating regret
WiFi Motion setupCompletion rate and time from intro to first test alertThe current flow can fail if the app closes

Guardrails, which must not get worse as any of this ships:

  • Support contacts about Shield, and repeat contacts within 7 days
  • People turning off alerts or WiFi Motion entirely
  • Cancellations and refunds in the first 30 days after upgrading, so a better-timed offer isn't just a pushier one
  • Uninstalls and App Store rating

How I built it

Research to live prototype in two days

I worked as a designer-builder, with Claude Code as the build partner.

  • Research. I captured the live app, gathered reviews and launch coverage, pulled 1,500 App Store reviews across Xfinity, Ring and SimpliSafe, and used Claude to sort them into themes. I checked each theme back against the source text and dropped claims the reviews didn't support.
  • Recreate. The current screens were rebuilt from my screenshots, so each change can be compared with what ships today.
  • Design in code. The prototype is one HTML file with no framework. I directed it screen by screen, sending annotated screenshots for padding, alignment, icons and type.
  • Ship. It deploys to Cloudflare from GitHub on every push, so each round of feedback was live in about a minute.

What I'd do next: test the pause and alert flows with five parents, confirm with engineering that setup can finish in the background, and take the privacy screen to legal, since every statement on it is a proposal.
