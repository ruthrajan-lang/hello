## Reflection

Reading this back was humbling. During the call it felt warm and easy, but on paper I can see how much space I took up.

**What went well.** We built rapport fast. She talked openly for almost an hour and ended by recommending other libraries I should visit, which is great for my fieldwork. My best moments were the shortest ones: "How so?", "What do you mean by that?", and asking for a specific example ("is there a recent time you stepped in?"). Those got me the best stories, like the Goosebumps request and the bookmobile. I also asked about her words instead of assuming (patrons, LPA), and I tried to anchor questions in the places I'd actually seen at the branch.

**What I need to work on.** The big one is that I talk too much. A lot of my turns were long summaries where I explained what her experience meant ("third spaces," "close-knit," "levels the playing field"). Twice she either repeated my framing back to me or politely corrected it. "Compassionate direction" is so much better than my "bring people down to earth." I also praised her a lot ("awesome," "I admire that," "really well done") and told stories about myself (my dad, sewing, dioramas, my job). I think I do this to show I'm engaged, but it pulls the focus to me and might signal what kinds of answers I want.

I also missed a lot of follow-ups on the most interesting stuff: "some days you just want to quit," the staffing cuts and restructuring, worrying about kids who haven't eaten, AI at the reference desk, and her theory about city vs. suburban libraries. Sometimes I skipped these to ask logistical questions I could have looked up. The moment I feel worst about is when she told me patrons had come in with suicidal thoughts and I said "that's so sweet." It didn't match what she'd just shared, and I wasn't prepared for something that serious.

Smaller things: I asked double questions, listed possible answers inside my questions, used words like "evangelized," and had a lot of false starts, which tells me I was planning my next question instead of listening.

**Next time I want to:**
- Talk less, and get comfortable with silence instead of filling it.
- Keep a "come back to this" list during the interview so I don't lose threads.
- Ask one question and stop.
- Swap praise for neutral stuff like "tell me more."
- Think ahead about how I'll respond if someone shares something heavy.
- Save my own analysis for fieldnotes.
- Explain the purpose of the project more clearly at the start.

I'd like to do a follow-up with her about staffing, burnout, and the mental health side of her work, since those felt like the heart of what she was telling me.
